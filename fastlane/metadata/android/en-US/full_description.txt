MetadataScrubber shows what a photo carries besides the picture itself, lets you choose what to remove, and then reads the new file back so you can see what is left. GPS position, camera serial numbers, owner names, dates, a built-in preview image and more.

It declares no Android permissions at all. There is no internet permission, so it cannot send your photos anywhere, and no storage permission either: pictures come in through the system file picker or a share from your gallery, and go out through the system save screen, a share or the clipboard. Check it yourself before installing:

    aapt dump permissions <the APK you downloaded>

The only line you should see is the package name.

- Shows before it removes. Every detail found in the file is listed with its value, grouped in a Red, an Amber and a Green section. Each section starts closed and says how many details it holds.
- Colour tiers guide the choice. Red means remove before sharing publicly, amber means think about it, green means harmless and useful. Only red details are ticked for removal to start with. Amber (dates, time zone, camera, lens and editing software) is kept unless you tick it, and so is green, which holds rotation, the colour profile, size and exposure. Captions, titles, descriptions, keywords, comments and Content Credentials are red, because they can name people. Amber keeps a date or device name only in the form it should have: text hidden after it, in a field the page does not show, or in the file's own structure is a red detail and is removed. Green keeps only numbers, fixed words and well-known profile names: a name hidden in a colour profile or a technical field is a red detail of its own, and removing it leaves the colours unchanged.
- HDR photos keep their gain map by default, so Ultra HDR JPEGs and iPhone HDR JPEG and HEIC photos stay bright on HDR screens. The gain map keeps only what it needs to work, and an iPhone photo keeps just the two HDR numbers of its maker notes: anything else in it or around it is still listed and removed. Tick HDR gain map to remove it. When your choices remove the colour profile or the gain map, a note says colours may look a little duller and bright areas less vivid on HDR screens.
- XMP, IPTC and PNG text that stay are always written again in a standard form, so their layout cannot carry hidden information.
- Your choice, one colour or one detail at a time. Tick a whole colour at once, or open it to choose single details.
- Lossless by default. Unless you crop, resize, convert or remove a sideways rotation, the picture itself is not saved again, so its pixels stay identical.
- Crop, resize and convert when you need to, for JPEG, PNG and WebP.
- Reads the result back, so the list shows what remains in the new file.
- Neutral file names. The phone's file name, which carries the date and time, is never reused. Results are named like image.public.jpg.
- Save, Copy or Share. Copy puts it on the clipboard without file details for 2 minutes; Share opens the share sheet, with a note under it: once a picture is online, you cannot take it back. Photos can also be shared in from your gallery.

Supported formats: JPEG, PNG, WebP and HEIC. HEIC metadata can be removed, but cropping and resizing HEIC are not available in the app, because Android's web engine cannot open HEIC pictures.

What it cannot remove: anything visible in the picture, the file name you choose, a camera's sensor fingerprint, and invisible watermarks woven into the pixels.

The app runs the same files as the website at metadata.stormberry.as, bundled inside it and working offline. The build checks that every one of them is byte for byte identical to the website's copy, apart from one added line in index.html that loads a small Android bridge for saving and sharing.

Privacy page: metadata.stormberry.as/privacy.html, also in the app.

This is a functioning prototype from Stormberry Labs, MIT licensed. Check the read-back list before you share a file.
